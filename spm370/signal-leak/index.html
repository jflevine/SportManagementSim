The final is still on air

“That clip in our Discord? That’s my home address.” Ari is one game from winning a volunteer-run college final. A small group in the fan community has been targeting players. The captain wants to play on.

You direct the tournament. You can pause the match and instruct the broadcast crew. Ari can tell you what they need. Campus safety handles physical-safety concerns. The crew is waiting on your first instruction. What goes first?

All characters are fictional adults. The address is withheld. Use invented details only. Ask your instructor privately for an alternative if needed.

How to play and save

Discord is the event’s group chat. A clip is a short saved video. The overlay displays chat messages on the broadcast. You need no game knowledge or outside research.

Read the team’s messages, consult a record or ask a private question. Choose an instruction and give a reason. The case clock moves with instructions and the advance button. Reading and typing take no case time.

Allow 3 minutes to orient, 10 to respond and 7–12 to write. Finish with one three-paragraph handoff (about 200–300 words) plus its automatic decision log, saved together in one text file.

Refresh this tab to resume. Closing it can lose the run. Nothing is sent to your instructor. On a shared device, keep your own copy, erase this run and remove your downloaded file before handing over the device.
