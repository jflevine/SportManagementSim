Your job and the setting

You are the tournament director, not an investigator or platform moderator. A collegiate esports final is a competition between college teams playing a video game. No knowledge of Circuit Break or esports rules is needed. Your goal is to make a defensible safeguarding response while important facts remain uncertain.

Safeguarding means taking practical steps to protect people from harm. Doxxing is exposing someone’s private identifying or location information. A chat overlay puts live chat messages onto the video broadcast. A clip is a saved portion of that video: deleting a chat message does not erase a recording or someone else’s repost.

What you know when you begin

A player reports that their residence location appeared in the Twitch broadcast. Production deleted the chat message but the overlay is still active. The captain wants to continue the final and has not seen the clip. The game has an enthusiastic fan community; a small group has crossed into targeted harassment. All people, locations and policies in this case are fictional. Do not invent a diagnosis, motive, threat, policy or legal finding.

Work through the incident

  1. Start with a fictional alias. Read the incoming messages and the three records under Records and authority. They contain the policy and role information you need; no outside research or real social-media account is required.
  2. Use Ask a person to clarify facts or needs. The characters use prepared responses, not open-ended AI. If a reply is limited, consult the records. Questions alone do not issue instructions.
  3. Use Direct an action when you decide to act. State the reason, responsible role and next check where requested. You do not need to click every action. Preserve uncertainty rather than treating concern as proof.
  4. Read each new update and reconsider your plan. The fictional clock advances when you act, not while you read or type. Advance to next update intentionally moves the case forward; it is not a skip to the answer. Both later updates must be seen before you finalize.
  5. Write the single three-part handoff below. Compare verified facts with unknowns, explain your actions, and identify remaining risks for the team and event organizer. Aim for about 200–300 words total. You are assessed on reasoning and the decision record, not a hidden score or predetermined legal conclusion.
