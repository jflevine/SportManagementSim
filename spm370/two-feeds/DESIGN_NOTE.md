# Design note — Two Feeds (SPM 370)

Written before building, as section 1 of `simkit/AUTHORING_GUIDE.md` requires. Jeff approved
proceeding without a reply, so every assumption is listed at the end for his review.

**Assignment.** A playable 20-minute activity. Pods advise an esports tournament organizer
after two parties claim conflicting broadcast rights.

## 1. The professional and the moment
Business & Legal Affairs Associate at Ninth Street Events (fictional organizer). Thursday,
3:00 PM. The Grand Final is Saturday at 6:00 PM. Lumen Live has written demanding
confirmation by end of day that nobody else will broadcast it. Parallax Network says it has a
signed right to stream it and its promo goes out at 8:00 PM. The CEO, Imani Cole, is in
meetings until 7:00 PM and will send whatever the associate advises.

## 2. The skill
- Read two agreements against each other and find the clause that decides the conflict.
- Notice what the documents do not say, and ask the one question that fills the gap.
- Give a client an instruction complete enough to act on, knowing she will do exactly that.

## 3. Who holds what
| Person | Wants | Fears | Knows that others do not | On their mind today |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Imani Cole, CEO | One instruction she can send | Losing a fee or a sponsor; being the last to know | She signed Lumen; she never approved Parallax | Two partners, one final |
| Grace Oyelaran, Lumen Live | The English audience she sold an advertiser | A surprise on Saturday | Lumen would not mind a Spanish stream with notice | Waiting for confirmation |
| Mateo Rivas, Parallax | The final on his channel | Cancelling after the teaser | His cast is in Spanish; he would like an English pre-show | Promo at 8:00 PM |
| Devon Hale, Partnerships | Not to be the story | Losing his job | He signed without authority and never told Mateo about Lumen | Hoping nobody asks who approved it |

## 4. What can be verified, and where
- **Lumen agreement:** exclusivity is defined as English-language (1.1, 2.1); other languages are allowed on 48 hours' written notice (2.3); a 40% fee reduction if English airs elsewhere or notice is missed (9.2). It does not say what Parallax plans.
- **Parallax agreement:** a non-exclusive stream right (3), subject to existing commitments the organizer had to disclose in writing (7); refund if withdrawn (9). It says nothing about language.
- **Signing policy:** Devon had no authority to sign. It does not say what follows from that.
- **Publisher licence:** consent covers only Lumen's English exclusivity; non-exclusive sublicenses need none.
- **Email thread:** "You're good for finals." Nothing about Lumen or language.
- **In no document:** the language of Parallax's stream, and Mateo's wish for an English pre-show.

## 5. Defensible moves that go somewhere uncomfortable
1. Enforce Lumen's exclusivity on 2.1 and Parallax 7. Sound on the paper; costs the $30,000 sponsor, who then points out the cast was Spanish all along.
2. Let both proceed because both are signed. Costs $48,000 when no notice is sent and an English pre-show airs.
3. Find the Spanish cast, advise coexistence, and forget the notice or the language condition. The CEO sends exactly what she was given.
4. Reassure a partner before advising the client, as Devon did in August.

## 6. Who relies on the decision
Imani sends what she is told, Thursday evening. Mateo publishes promo and, absent a
restriction, runs his English pre-show on Saturday. Grace either accepts the notice or
invokes 9.2. Students see all of it after sending their advice.

## 7. Interaction format, and why
A **reading table**: two documents, or a document and a conversation, side by side. Students
pin clauses as the basis for advice. Reading costs 20 minutes of a three-hour afternoon and a
message 15, so a pod cannot read everything and ask everyone at leisure; the 48-hour notice
window closes at 6:00 PM. Conversation is secondary: it exists because the deciding fact is
in nobody's paper. The **advice form** is the submission, and the CEO acts on it literally.
This is neither The Report's inbox nor The Shortfall's board.

## 8. What the simulation remembers
Facts each person holds; which documents were read and when; pinned clauses (in the ledger,
so a pod shares them and the instructor sees them live); reassurances given to either
partner, in the student's words; whether 6:00 PM has passed; the advice as sent.

## 9. The debrief
**Side by side:** Team Anchor (read both agreements, enforced exclusivity, asked no one) and
Team Beacon (asked Mateo what he planned to stream, found 2.3, advised notice and a language
condition). **Evidence on screen:** each team's advice in its own words, the clauses each
pinned, whether each learned the cast was Spanish and from whom, and what Lumen, Parallax and
Ninth Street each ended up with. **Question:** *Both teams read the same two agreements and
gave Imani opposite instructions. Which clause is each team standing on, and what did each
one have to assume about a fact neither agreement states?*

## Assumptions for Jeff to confirm
1. **Students are business-side advisers, not counsel.** Nothing asks what a court would do. Authority to sign, reliance and remedies are raised as debrief questions, not answered.
2. **The clause wording is mine.** It is written to be readable in minutes, not to mirror a real media-rights agreement. Tell me where it teaches a wrong habit.
3. **The numbers are invented:** $120,000 fee, 40% reduction, $30,000 sponsorship, 48 hours' notice.
4. **Language as the dividing line** (English-exclusive, other languages on notice) is a plausible drafting device I chose because it puts the deciding fact outside the documents. I did not check it against your chapter; I worked only from the repository, as the test required.
5. **One pod, one seat,** with teammates able to join from other devices.
6. **Twenty minutes** assumes pods read two or three documents, ask one or two questions and advise. Untested with students.
7. **No "correct" answer is displayed.** Coexistence with notice and a language condition costs nothing in this fiction, but enforcement is defensible and the debrief treats it that way.
