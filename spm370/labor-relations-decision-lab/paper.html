Pixel Forge: Practice Case

Practice only: This preserved paper activity does not contain the graded MC assessment and cannot submit work or produce a course grade. Complete the online graded assessment for a server-confirmed receipt.

15–18 minutes. Choose one role only: management adviser OR union adviser. Read that role’s section below. Open/read two evidence files, select one first recommendation, then reveal the new facts and select one response. Write your four-part memo. No correct-click score.

Shared case: Pixel Forge owns a game and directly employs the covered U.S. professional players in this case. Their union is already the lawfully recognized exclusive representative. No collective bargaining agreement has been reached. Management has canceled three meetings, said it cannot afford raises, and refused the union’s request for supporting financial information.

Employee coverage, U.S. jurisdiction, employer identity, the bargaining unit, and lawful recognition are stipulated. They are not conclusions about real esports publishers. All added documents, numbers, private briefings, and consequences are fictional teaching extensions of lecture slides 23–24 and chapter Hypothetical 5.

Output: Two distinct legal risks, one missing fact (source and why it matters), and a concrete 48-hour recommendation with a tradeoff. Use one or two concise sentences per answer. Save or share only as your instructor directs. This page collects or submits nothing.

Rules you may use

Start with the legal relationship

A contract label does not decide employee status. The actual working relationship, relevant employer, appropriate unit, and U.S. statutory jurisdiction matter. Publisher IP ownership and tournament control do not by themselves establish employer or joint-employer status. Those threshold issues are stipulated in this case.

Chapter pp. 2–3, 12–15, 26–28 · Slides 5–6, 11, 25, 28

Section 7 can apply before recognition

Covered employees can act together about pay and working conditions without first winning a union election. An individual may act for coworkers or seek group action. Purpose, manner, and context matter. Both employer retaliation and union coercion can raise legal issues.

Chapter pp. 8–11, 21–26 · Slides 7–9, 19–20

Representation is not a contract

Voluntary recognition based on majority employee support and the election route are distinct. A 30% showing can support an election petition; it does not win the election. To win representation in an election, the union generally needs a majority of valid votes cast. An association’s name alone proves neither exclusive status nor a CBA. Recognition is already settled here.

Chapter pp. 12–15, 27–28 · Slides 10–13

Good faith is a process obligation

Both sides must meet at reasonable times and bargain genuinely over mandatory subjects. Neither must agree or make a concession. Assess the overall course of conduct. A deadline, a rejected proposal, or two unsuccessful meetings does not itself establish impasse or unlawful surface bargaining.

Chapter pp. 16–18 · Slides 14–17 · NLRA §§ 8(a)(5), 8(b)(3), 8(d)

Financial information is fact-specific

A genuine inability-to-pay claim may call for relevant substantiation in response to the union’s request. Mere unwillingness is different, but wording alone is not decisive. Consider the actual claim, relevant period, requested records, and confidentiality accommodation. This does not create a blanket right to every record or identical financial duties on both sides.

Chapter pp. 16–18, 31 · Slide 16 · NLRB v. Truitt

The union represents the whole unit

The exclusive representative owes fair representation to all unit employees, including nonmembers. The standard is arbitrary, discriminatory, or bad-faith conduct. A union retains lawful bargaining discretion: it need not secure everyone’s preferred outcome or pursue every grievance. A reasoned tradeoff is not automatically a breach.

Chapter pp. 19–20, 25–26 · Slides 18, 20 · Vaca and O’Neill

A charge begins a process

An unfair-labor-practice charge alleges misconduct. Investigation, potential settlement or complaint, adjudication, and review follow as applicable. A charge is not a finding or an automatic wage award. Remedies depend on the proven violation; bargaining, notice, reinstatement, or backpay remedies may be appropriate. A contract or concession is not guaranteed.

Chapter pp. 21–25, 31 · Slides 21–24

Economic pressure is not an automatic safe harbor

A strike is a withholding of labor; a lockout is a withholding of work. Lawfulness and protection depend on the purpose, timing, conduct, and applicable restrictions; economic and unfair-labor-practice strikes can have different consequences. Do not infer immunity from a vote or allegation. Contract/IP questions and other countries’ laws need separate analysis.

Chapter pp. 10–11, 26–30 · Slides 9, 25–28

Management adviser

Your client: Pixel Forge management. Keep the business viable while repairing the bargaining process. Protect operational flexibility without bypassing the representative or retaliating against players.

You advise the employer. You may recommend a firm wage position and alternatives. You cannot treat ownership of the game, the absence of a CBA, or financial pressure as permission to ignore bargaining duties. Your goal is a defensible process, not defeating the union.

Circle your priority: Cash stability (Avoid promises the business cannot fund.) / Player retention (Protect trust and keep talent without individual end-runs.) / Operational continuity (Keep schedules workable and decisions explainable.)

1. Evidence · Choose two

□ Canceled-meeting record

Three scheduled meetings were canceled by management. Travel was cited once, a sponsor emergency once, and no reason once. Only one replacement date has been offered. The complete history of both sides’ availability is not in this file.

Limit/meaning: Operational reasons are relevant, but so are timing, repeated delay, and concrete attempts to reschedule. Attendance or cancellation counts alone do not decide good faith.

□ Your financial briefing

Your bargaining representative said, “We cannot afford raises,” and added, “Investors will not approve more spending.” The union requested recent operating results and projected payroll costs supporting the affordability claim. Management rejected the request without addressing relevance or confidentiality.

Limit/meaning: Find out what the business actually claims. Investor preference and financial inability are not interchangeable. A blanket refusal after an inability claim creates a distinct concern.

□ Proposal and business limits

The union seeks 6% higher salaries, weekly rest, and earlier travel notice. Management wants predictable availability and an affordable payroll. Someone also proposes a veto over entry into a new game title. No terms are agreed and no impasse has been established.

Limit/meaning: Pay and working time are core mandatory topics. Analyze business-direction decisions separately from their employment effects. No-deal status does not itself authorize unilateral changes.

□ Roster-retention notes

The stipulated unit includes starters and reserve players, both union members and nonmembers. Stars emphasize earnings and career options. Several lower-paid reserves emphasize stable pay, rest, and travel notice. Management worries about losing stars and asks about private retention offers.

Limit/meaning: Different priorities can support reasoned proposals through the union. Do not use individual arrangements to evade collective representation or tie opportunities to abandoning protected activity.

2. First advice · Choose one

Agenda:

Mandatory: The union seeks a 6% salary increase, weekly rest, and earlier notice of required travel. These concern pay, hours, and working conditions. Both sides must bargain in good faith, not necessarily agree.

Separate the decision from its effects: A proposal about entering a new game title raises a business-direction question. Do not automatically treat that decision as mandatory; employment effects may require separate bargaining.

A legal limit: Neither side can make unlawful retaliation or coercion lawful by agreeing to it. Recognition also does not guarantee a CBA.

Players’ group discussion of pay or workload may be protected under Section 7. Explaining proposals is not automatically direct dealing, but seeking separate bargains over represented terms can be. Do not punish protected activity.

□ Hold the wage position; repair the process

Offer prompt, firm meeting dates. Explain the current pay proposal, clarify the affordability claim, and promptly assess the union’s targeted request.

□ Develop a phased package through the union

Offer replacement dates and explore a funded pay phase, rest, and travel notice while promptly addressing the financial request. Make no promise before costing it.

□ Resolve pay through individual star deals

Offer selected players private increases if they abandon group demands, and leave the union’s financial request unanswered.

□ Accept 6% because the law demands a concession

Recommend the entire raise now on the theory that disagreement and three cancellations legally require acceptance.

Pause: Record your choice before reading the new facts. Explain the legal reason and practical cost in one sentence.

3. New facts · Then choose one response

Your internal CFO memo says: “We cannot fund the proposed increase during the next six months unless a delayed sponsor payment arrives.” Cash-flow and payroll projections exist. A sponsor contract has confidential commercial terms. Management has now communicated the six-month explanation to the union, which offers to discuss limited disclosure and protections.

Your client wants to retain scarce talent but cannot responsibly promise money that is unavailable. Star-only private deals would leave the collective bargaining problem unresolved. No one has yet agreed to a disclosure protocol or pay package.

□ Provide relevant support with workable safeguards

Promptly discuss the period, records, redactions, and access protections. Keep the meetings and a firm, accurately explained wage position while considering counters.

□ Offer independent review and a contingent proposal

Propose a prompt review by an agreed adviser under suitable confidentiality terms, alongside a clearly costed sponsor-payment contingency and near-term rest/travel measures.

□ Rename inability as unwillingness and deny the request

Replace “cannot afford” with “will not pay” while leaving the underlying cash facts and prior refusal unchanged.

□ Declare impasse and implement a pay policy tomorrow

Treat the stalled talks and sponsor deadline as enough to impose new terms without resolving the information request.

4. Adviser memo

Bargaining or information risk: Analyze the canceled meetings and/or information refusal using a case fact and an uncertainty.

Distinct risk for your client: Identify a distinct legal concern tied to the record or a proposed response: information refusal (if Risk 1 concerns meetings), direct dealing, retaliation, or premature unilateral change. Distinguish something that occurred from a risk to prevent.

One missing fact: Name a fact still needed, where to obtain it, and how it could change your advice.

48-hour recommendation and tradeoff: Give management a concrete next step for the next 48 hours. Connect it to your chosen priority, one tradeoff, and what the union reasonably needs.

Optional: What is the other side’s strongest legitimate concern?

After completing: feedback for this role

Hold the wage position; repair the process

This addresses meetings and information separately. Keeping a wage position is not itself bad faith. You still need genuine engagement, accurate financial statements, and a timely, adequate response to information the union is entitled to receive.

Tradeoff: Preserves immediate cash flexibility, but players may see little economic movement. Credibility depends on what management actually does next.

Develop a phased package through the union

A package can support genuine negotiation without guaranteeing agreement. Potential movement does not replace any duty to substantiate an actual inability claim or cure past conduct. Bargain through the recognized representative.

Tradeoff: May improve retention and predictability, but adds costing work and could limit flexibility. The union can reject or counter it.

Resolve pay through individual star deals

This proposal raises direct-dealing and interference concerns, beyond the existing meeting and information issues. Direct communication is not always unlawful, but individual bargains over represented terms and conditions tied to abandoning protected activity are different.

Tradeoff: Could produce short-term agreement with a few stars while increasing legal exposure, mistrust, and division across the unit.

Accept 6% because the law demands a concession

The law does not compel a concession. Management can voluntarily accept a proposal if authorized and feasible, but the stated legal reason is mistaken. An agreement would not automatically resolve every issue concerning prior conduct.

Tradeoff: Might satisfy some pay interests, but an uncosted commitment could endanger cash stability. You still need to repair process and information handling.

Provide relevant support with workable safeguards

The substantive six-month inability claim strengthens the need to address relevant substantiation. Genuine confidentiality calls for a workable accommodation, not an automatic exemption. The adequacy of the response still depends on facts and scope.

Tradeoff: Protects the credibility of the cash position, but uses staff time and requires sharing relevant evidence under appropriate protections. No agreement is assured.

Offer independent review and a contingent proposal

A mutually workable review arrangement may accommodate confidentiality. It cannot be imposed as a device to stall or withhold relevant support. The union may reject the method or the contingent proposal; bargaining must remain genuine.

Tradeoff: May protect commercial details and open options, but adds coordination and leaves players bearing some payment uncertainty. Avoid an indefinite review timeline.

Rename inability as unwillingness and deny the request

A truthful, properly communicated clarification or withdrawal can matter. Simply relabeling this documented capacity problem does not resolve the prior claim, relevant request, or other information obligations.

Tradeoff: Avoids disclosure work briefly, but a contradiction can deepen mistrust and increase the need to explain the record.

Declare impasse and implement a pay policy tomorrow

Impasse is a fact-specific assessment of the bargaining history and genuine exhaustion of prospects. Unanswered relevant information and other bargaining problems can matter. Unilateral changes before satisfying applicable duties may create additional exposure.

Tradeoff: Creates apparent certainty quickly, but may worsen the dispute and require later corrective action. This exercise supplies no basis to guarantee lawful implementation.

Union adviser

Your client: the players’ union. Protect player interests and informed bargaining while representing the entire unit fairly. Preserve leverage without confusing a demand, a charge, or a threatened action with a legal victory.

You advise the recognized exclusive union, not just its highest-paid members. You may maintain a wage demand, propose alternatives, or recommend investigating a charge. Your own bargaining conduct and treatment of all unit employees matter.

Circle your priority: Compensation (Seek sustainable pay gains and credible financial evidence.) / Job and income stability (Protect lower-paid and short-career players from avoidable uncertainty.) / Workload and voice (Improve rest, travel notice, and representation across the unit.)

1. Evidence · Choose two

□ Canceled-meeting record

Management canceled three scheduled meetings, citing travel once, a sponsor emergency once, and no reason once. It offered one replacement date. Your union has asked for firm dates. You do not have the full record of both sides’ availability.

Limit/meaning: Document reasonable dates and responses. The pattern raises concern, but legitimate reasons and the full bargaining history still matter. A charge would not convert these facts into a finding.

□ Management’s financial statement

At bargaining, management said, “We cannot afford raises,” then, “Investors will not approve more spending.” Your union requested recent operating results and projected payroll costs supporting affordability. Management refused. You have not seen internal cash records.

Limit/meaning: Tie the request to the actual position and bargaining need. Claimed inability can make substantiation relevant; it is not a right to an unrestricted corporate audit. Do not pretend you know what unopened records show.

□ Proposal and negotiating space

Your proposal seeks 6% higher salaries, weekly rest, and earlier travel notice. You have not promised to concede any term. A player also wants a veto over entering new game titles. No agreement or established impasse exists.

Limit/meaning: Pay and working conditions are mandatory topics. A business-direction decision and its employee effects require separate analysis. A lawful optional topic cannot simply become a condition for all bargaining.

□ Players’ competing priorities

The stipulated unit includes starters and reserves, both members and nonmembers. Some stars prioritize an immediate raise. Several lower-paid reserves prioritize stable income, rest, and travel notice. Two nonmembers have sent scheduling concerns but have not yet been consulted.

Limit/meaning: Fair representation extends to everyone in the unit. A reasoned balance can favor some terms over others, but nonmembership, dissent, or favoritism is not a sound basis to refuse representation.

2. First advice · Choose one

Agenda:

Mandatory: The union seeks a 6% salary increase, weekly rest, and earlier notice of required travel. These concern pay, hours, and working conditions. Both sides must bargain in good faith, not necessarily agree.

Separate the decision from its effects: A proposal about entering a new game title raises a business-direction question. Do not automatically treat that decision as mandatory; employment effects may require separate bargaining.

A legal limit: Neither side can make unlawful retaliation or coercion lawful by agreeing to it. Recognition also does not guarantee a CBA.

The union represents members and nonmembers in the stipulated unit. It can make reasoned tradeoffs without securing everyone’s favorite outcome. Arbitrary, discriminatory, or bad-faith representation is different from an unpopular but rational judgment.

□ Keep the wage demand and build the record

Maintain 6%, propose firm meeting dates, and renew a targeted request tied to the inability claim. Explain the bargaining needs and invite a workable confidentiality discussion.

□ Explore a package while preserving the request

Discuss a phased wage proposal with rest and travel improvements, consult starters and reserves, and continue pressing for relevant financial support.

□ Condition every meeting on a full audit and veto

Decline all further meetings until management grants unrestricted books access and a veto over its next game-title expansion.

□ Withdraw because no CBA means no rights

Tell players the union has no bargaining authority until a contract is signed, and stop pursuing meetings or supporting information.

Pause: Record your choice before reading the new facts. Explain the legal reason and practical cost in one sentence.

3. New facts · Then choose one response

Management now states in writing: “We cannot fund the proposed increase during the next six months unless a delayed sponsor payment arrives.” It says cash-flow and payroll projections exist but raises commercial confidentiality concerns. Your union has offered to discuss limited disclosure and protections. You have not yet inspected those projections or the sponsor contract.

Player priorities now diverge. Some stars want immediate wage pressure; several reserves favor near-term rest and pay stability. A union officer suggests ignoring the two nonmembers’ scheduling concerns. They are employees in the represented unit. You have made no decision about their concerns yet.

□ Test the claim and represent the whole unit

Seek timely records supporting the six-month claim, discuss suitable safeguards, keep bargaining dates, and assess starters’, reserves’, and nonmembers’ concerns on their merits.

□ Evaluate a charge while continuing to bargain

Ask labor counsel to assess the meeting and refusal record, preserve evidence, renew the focused request, and maintain substantive talks and fair consideration of all unit employees.

□ Reserve representation for dues-paying stars

Refuse to consider the nonmembers’ scheduling concerns and let influential starters decide which unit employees receive help.

□ Promise that an immediate walkout is legally risk-free

Recommend stopping work now on the theory that management’s refusal automatically proves an unfair labor practice and guarantees protection and reinstatement.

Bargaining or information risk: Analyze management’s meeting and/or information conduct using a case fact, without treating an allegation as a finding.

Distinct risk for your client: Identify a distinct risk your union client should prevent, such as unfair representation, coercion, an overbroad bargaining condition, or an unsafe claim about strike protection. Distinguish a proposed action from something already done.

One missing fact: Name a fact still needed, where to obtain it, and how it could change your advice.

48-hour recommendation and tradeoff: Give the union a concrete next step for the next 48 hours. Connect it to your chosen priority, one tradeoff, and what management reasonably needs.

After completing: feedback for this role

Keep the wage demand and build the record

You do not have to concede. A relevant, focused request and reasonable meeting proposals help test the employer’s claim and document the process. Do not treat the initial statement as proof that every financial record must be produced.

Tradeoff: Preserves wage leverage, but resolution may take time. Make sure lower-paid players’ immediate needs and differing views are heard.

Explore a package while preserving the request

Bargaining discretion allows rational tradeoffs. Consultation can inform fair representation, but no single poll or unhappy player determines legality. Exploring terms does not waive the request merely by itself or require the employer to concede.

Tradeoff: Could address immediate working conditions and stability, but some players may view a phased raise as too weak. Explain the rationale across the unit.

Condition every meeting on a full audit and veto

Financial scope must relate to the actual claim and bargaining need. Refusing genuine engagement or insisting to impasse on a permissive subject can raise separate concerns. The legality of a specific request or decision still depends on its substance and context.

Tradeoff: Signals pressure, but can stall useful talks, weaken the record of reasonable engagement, and overshadow the strongest information claim.

Withdraw because no CBA means no rights

The union is already the stipulated exclusive representative. The absence of a first CBA does not erase bargaining duties or covered employees’ Section 7 rights. A union can choose a strategy, but this legal premise is incorrect.

Tradeoff: Ends immediate negotiating effort while leaving pay, scheduling, and the representation mandate unaddressed.

Test the claim and represent the whole unit

The concrete inability statement strengthens the relevance of supporting information, but not unlimited disclosure. The union must fairly represent all unit employees. It can make a rational tradeoff after assessing facts; it need not secure every requested benefit.

Tradeoff: Builds a stronger factual basis and legitimacy, but takes time and may disappoint people seeking immediate pay or action.

Evaluate a charge while continuing to bargain

A charge can trigger investigation; it is not a finding or an automatic raise. Its merits and any remedies depend on the evidence and process. Continuing genuine bargaining does not require abandoning a potential claim or making a concession.

Tradeoff: May address serious process concerns while increasing time, cost, and tension. Set realistic expectations about relief and keep the client’s practical priorities in view.

Reserve representation for dues-paying stars

Fair representation covers all unit employees, including nonmembers. A union has discretion to make reasoned judgments and need not pursue every claim, but refusing representation because of nonmembership or favoritism can raise a serious issue.

Tradeoff: May please influential supporters briefly while damaging legitimacy and exposing the union to an additional dispute.

Promise that an immediate walkout is legally risk-free

A charge or allegation is not a finding. A prior Board ruling is not required for a strike to be protected. Protection and reinstatement consequences depend on the action’s object, timing, manner, applicable restrictions, and whether the dispute is economic or involves unfair labor practices. Do not promise immunity from the facts given.

Tradeoff: Economic pressure may be powerful, but players may face income loss and uncertainty. Investigate and obtain advice before promising safety or outcomes.

Before you finish

A charge alleges misconduct; it does not decide the case, guarantee a raise, or automatically secure an injunction. Both parties may bargain firmly without conceding. Distinguish something that happened from a risk your advice seeks to prevent. This fictional exercise is not legal advice.
