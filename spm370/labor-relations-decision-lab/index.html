BARGAINING ROOM

Instructor guide · public teaching notes

Lead the debrief.

The task is a defensible next step, not a verdict or a forced deal.

Run it in 15–18 minutes

  1. 2 minutes: Teams of 3–4 read the brief and assign roles.
  2. 3 minutes: Inspect two evidence files. Name one fact still missing.
  3. 3 minutes: Choose a bargaining response and read its feedback.
  4. 3 minutes: Respond to the financial twist.
  5. 4 minutes: Draft two risks, one missing fact, and the next step.
  6. Up to 3 minutes: Compare recommendations in class.

One device per team, or project the activity and take class votes. No timer locks, grades, names, sign-in, or response collection.

What a strong recommendation does

  • Identifies a possible failure to meet and confer in good faith, using the pattern and rescheduling record.
  • Separately identifies the information-refusal risk tied to the actual inability claim and relevant request.
  • Explains that hard bargaining is permitted and a raise is not compulsory.
  • Calls for prompt meetings, clarification, relevant substantiation, and a workable confidentiality accommodation.
  • States what remains uncertain instead of declaring liability.
Suggested model response and misconceptions

Risk 1: Three cancellations and limited replacement dates could support a bad-faith bargaining allegation when viewed with the full record. Legitimate reasons and meaningful efforts to reschedule matter; the cancellations alone do not decide the case.

Risk 2: Refusing relevant financial support after claiming an inability to fund raises may violate the bargaining obligation. The precise statements, request, financial period, relevance, and confidentiality concerns matter. An unwillingness to pay is different, but changing the label alone does not erase an actual inability claim or other information duties.

Missing fact: What did management actually communicate about its ability to pay during the proposed contract period, and what records substantiate that statement? Another sound answer asks for the complete scheduling record.

Next step: Offer firm replacement meeting dates immediately. Accurately clarify management’s financial position, promptly respond to the relevant request, and propose targeted disclosure and an appropriate confidentiality arrangement. Discuss compensation alternatives and scheduling without assuming a duty to concede.

Probe: “What changes if management has enough money but prefers to protect its target margin?” Then ask: “Would that eliminate every information duty?” (No; other relevant information obligations may still apply.)

Do not teach: “Three cancellations automatically violate the law,” “every money dispute requires every financial record,” “the union must accept any NDA,” or “good faith means accepting the raise.”

Debrief questions and optional assessment
  1. Which fact made you change your recommendation?
  2. Where did you separate a legal risk from a relationship or business cost?
  3. What evidence would weaken your bad-faith concern?
  4. What should management do in the next 48 hours, without promising a particular wage outcome?

Optional 4-point rubric: one point each for distinct risks; fact-sensitive inability/unwillingness analysis; a relevant missing fact; and a feasible process recommendation. Assess reasoning, not which button a team selected. The app does not grade free text or send it anywhere.

Pixel Forge: Bargaining Room

SPM 370 · Labor relations · 15–18 minute team decision lab

Work in teams of 3–4. Choose an operations adviser, union-perspective challenger, evidence lead, and reporter. Combine roles if needed.

The case

Pixel Forge is the stipulated employer of covered U.S. players. Their union is the recognized exclusive representative. Management cancels three bargaining meetings, says it cannot afford raises, and refuses the union’s request for supporting financial information. You are the operations adviser. Identify two legal risks and recommend the next step.

1. Inspect two files (3 minutes)

The following are fictional extensions of the lecture’s case. Choose two before reading them aloud.

  1. Meeting calendar: A travel conflict, sponsor emergency, and unexplained cancellation. One replacement date offered; no dates offered for the other two. The full bargaining history is missing.
  2. Financial exchange: Management says it cannot afford raises and investors will not approve more spending. The union asks for recent operating results and projected payroll costs to test that statement. Management refuses all supporting information.
  3. Union proposal: A 6% salary increase, guaranteed weekly rest, and earlier travel notice. The union says it will discuss sequencing and alternatives. No agreement yet.
  4. Sponsor headline: A new sponsorship is advertised. No public dollar figure, payment schedule, or restricted-use terms. A headline is not evidence of available payroll cash.

2. Choose a response (3 minutes)

A. Grant the entire raise to prove good faith.
B. Offer replacement meetings, clarify the financial position, and address the relevant information request while continuing bargaining.
C. Meet again and hold the wage position, but refuse every financial document as private.
D. Pause bargaining until sponsor talks end and announce the new pay policy directly to players.

Explain a legal risk and an operational consequence. A legally sound process does not guarantee agreement.

3. New fact (3 minutes)

The CFO writes: “We cannot fund the proposed increase during the next six months unless a delayed sponsor payment arrives.” Recent cash-flow and payroll projections exist. A sponsor contract contains confidential commercial terms. The union offers to discuss limited disclosure and confidentiality.

Choose: A. Promptly discuss targeted records, period, redactions, and confidentiality while keeping bargaining dates. B. Rename the claim “unwillingness” and deny everything. C. Grant 6% because the documents force a concession. D. Require an indefinite blanket NDA before considering any request. Explain what your choice fixes and what it does not resolve.

4. Team memo (4 minutes)

Risk 1, tied to a fact: ___________________________________________________

Risk 2, tied to a different fact: ____________________________________________

One missing fact and why it matters: _______________________________________

Your next step and tradeoff: ______________________________________________

Good faith does not compel agreement or concession. An actual inability-to-pay claim can call for substantiation in response to a relevant request; unwillingness is distinct. Cancellations require context. No single fact decides this case.
