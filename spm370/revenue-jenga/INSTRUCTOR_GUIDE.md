# Revenue Jenga — Instructor Guide

## Placement in the lecture
Use immediately after **“How Professional Esports Teams Get Paid”** and before **“Closed & Partnered Publisher Leagues.”**

The preceding slide establishes the modern pro-team mix: commercial revenue, publisher-linked support, and the distinction between capital and repeatable operating revenue. Revenue Jenga asks students to evaluate **relative stability**, not merely name revenue streams.

## Learning alignment
Primary: Chapter 3 Learning Outcome 2 — identify the primary revenue streams associated with publishers, leagues/tournament organizers, teams, and players, and evaluate their relative stability.

Secondary: Learning Outcome 4 — diagnose friction points and recommend practical business-response or risk-mitigation strategies.

## Suggested classroom timing (6–8 minutes)
1. **Role — 30 seconds.** Each pod chooses or randomizes publisher, pro team, tournament organizer, or player/creator.
2. **Build — 2 minutes.** Allocate exactly 100 revenue points. Do not tell students there is a “correct” mix.
3. **Shock — 15 seconds.** Have each pod choose 1–6 before clicking the matching sealed case.
4. **Rebuild — 2 minutes.** Pods get only 20 pivot points and must identify the new dependency and legal instrument that matters more.
5. **Report — 2–3 minutes.** Ask 2–3 pods to report role, top exposure, shock, pivot, and what became the new chokepoint.

## Intro language
“The last slide gave us the menu of ways esports organizations can make money. But identifying revenue streams is the easy part. The management question is how stable they are. You have 100 points. Build the business. Then I am going to remove part of it.”

## Shock deck
1. Sponsor Exit — sponsorship hit.
2. Revenue-Share Cut — publisher-linked/digital support hit.
3. Viewership Drop — media, sponsorship, event, and creator value soften.
4. Fail to Qualify — prize revenue disappears and visibility-linked streams weaken.
5. Platform Change — streaming/content monetization deteriorates.
6. New Compliance Cost — revenue remains but an unplanned operating burden reduces resilience.

## Debrief questions
- What looked like “revenue” before the shock but now looks like **dependency**?
- Did diversification actually reduce risk, or simply create more relationships to manage?
- Which replacement stream created the most important new legal or contractual dependency?
- Which model had the highest theoretical upside? Which had the most durable operating base?
- Why is outside investment not the same thing as repeatable operating revenue?

## Key teaching point
Revenue diversification does not eliminate dependency. It changes **which dependencies the organization must manage**. Sponsorship depends on sponsors and category rights; media depends on audience and distribution; publisher-linked revenue depends on publisher strategy; prize money depends on competitive performance; creator revenue depends on talent, platforms, and audience attention.

## Scoring note
The resilience score is intentionally a **discussion heuristic, not a grade or financial forecast**. It rewards remaining revenue, diversification, role fit, and a coherent response. Students should be encouraged to challenge the result if they can defend their business and legal reasoning.
