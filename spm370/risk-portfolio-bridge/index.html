Risk management before the next practice

Identify: name a specific event, the people exposed, and the facts you need. Assess: explain the likelihood and severity of a defined consequence over a stated period. Mitigate: compare workable controls, then assign an owner and review the result.

Preventive law brings legal questions into planning before a dispute. A reasonable precaution and a legally required duty are different questions. An organization’s specific services, relationships, and applicable rules matter. A written policy alone does not show that protection works in practice.

For example, promising supervision until pickup calls for checking who actually stays after a youth event ends. A trained lead and backup can close a staffing gap. Sign-out records help test whether the control worked.

Reporting routes must be accessible and backed by trained people. A route outside the coaching chain can matter when a concern involves a coach. Safeguarding guidance helps design protections, but does not by itself create a universal legal standard.

Urgent protection: a credible immediate threat or an applicable reporting obligation requires timely action. Do not wait for a matrix rating or a scheduled review to take a required protective step.

The case: late-night scrims

A collegiate esports team practices six days a week. Players regularly stay online past midnight to scrimmage teams in other regions. One player recently posted stressed and discouraged messages in the team Discord. The coach sees the posts and does not intervene because playoffs are approaching.

This is a fictional course scenario. Do not invent a diagnosis, age, injury, or school policy. Your job is to advise the program while accounting for the player’s perspective.

1. The decision and the people

Identify a specific risk event rather than a broad topic such as “mental health.” Use only the fictional facts. A scrim is a practice match against another team.

2. Two perspectives on the same event

Assess the next week if practice continues. Likelihood means the chance of your stated outcome, not how often practice occurs. Impact means the severity of that outcome. For likelihood, low means unusual or relatively unlikely conditions, medium means reasonably plausible, and high means recurring, already present, or especially likely. For impact, low means limited and easily corrected exposure, medium means meaningful financial, contractual, operational, or reputational consequences, and high means major liability, loss of rights, serious disruption, or serious stakeholder harm. Explain uncertainty rather than diagnosing a player or declaring liability.

3. Priority and legal inquiry

A concern can justify prompt action without establishing negligence. Verify duty, breach, causation, and legally recognized injury separately. The scenario supplies no age, diagnosis, injury record, jurisdiction, or school policy.

4. Two workable controls

Choose genuinely different actions. A control is a concrete response intended to reduce the likelihood or impact of harm. Name a person with authority to carry it out. Remaining risk is what could still happen after the action.

5. Your recommendation

Write a short recommendation for tonight. You may combine controls, but say what happens first. No single rating or control earns an automatic correct answer.

Worked comparison: open after writing your recommendation

One defensible player analysis identifies reduced opportunity for rest if late sessions continue. A provisional high likelihood and medium impact might fit that specific outcome, but actual effects remain unknown. Moving or cancelling the late block can improve the opportunity for rest. It may cost access to opponents, and unofficial practice could continue.

From the program’s perspective, a known concern may go unaddressed. A private check-in by an appropriate welfare contact can improve access to support. It takes staff time and trust, and does not change the schedule by itself. A director could prioritize changing the schedule while arranging a same-day private invitation to talk.

Check actual finish times and invite private feedback after a week. The aim is to test the controls, not to circulate sensitive disclosures. A different rating or ordering can be defensible if supported. These are provisional risk judgments, not findings of negligence.
