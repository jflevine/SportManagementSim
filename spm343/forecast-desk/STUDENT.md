# Forecast Desk: student instructions and text alternative

You advise Harbor Circuit, a fictional esports organization. Use the assigned Finch chapter and class concepts. Treat forecasts as possibilities to investigate, not current facts. No outside research, gaming expertise, personal data or account is required for this companion. Use any format your instructor approves if the interactive view is inaccessible.

This is ungraded planning. Complete the actual Knowledge Check in Canvas when your instructor posts it. Downloading notes is not submitting them. Your instructor supplies the deadline and submission route separately.

For each round, choose a path and note your initial assumption. Then read the update and explain whether you keep, change or defer your recommendation, one trade-off, and what evidence you need.

## 1. Technology
Harbor can test one idea for six weeks: phone-friendly competitions, a headset-based experience, or AI-assisted match-clip selection. Which would you test, and what must be true for it to help?

Update: a proposed partner cannot confirm one needed capability or permission. Identify that dependency for your chosen path. Revise the test so it does not promise an unavailable feature.

## 2. Audience
Harbor has a growing free audience. Choose a small paid-membership offer, a creator collaboration, or one local community event. What behavior would show the idea has value?

Update: attention rises but repeat participation stays flat. Would you repeat, change or stop? Identify one useful measure, including cost where relevant.

## 3. Safeguards
Choose one safeguard to strengthen: competition rules, reporting concerns, or moderation responsibilities.

Update: participants find one rule confusing and do not know the contact person for concerns. Name who owns the response and explain how your chosen safeguard works with the other two.

## Final planning memo
In four concise parts: choose one Finch trend and connect it to a course topic; name a segment and why it may grow; identify one unmet need; propose a small pilot and evidence for expanding, revising or stopping. Treat growth and need as hypotheses. This prepares the final Canvas response; do not write a second essay. Follow the Canvas question's length and submission instructions when available.

Download your notes before leaving. Clear the draft after using a shared device. Do not rely on private browsing or downloaded-file storage to save work permanently.
