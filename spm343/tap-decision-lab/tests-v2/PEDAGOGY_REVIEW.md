# Independent teaching review: TAP Decision Lab 2

## Intended decision structure

The case is a constrained event decision, not a proposal-ranking quiz: 24 visitors, 12 stations, two hosts, $300 and 90 minutes. Cup ($280; 12 stations), Open ($180; 10 stations) and Showcase ($240; eight stations) create distinct access, staffing, competitive, pacing and cost tradeoffs. The initial visitor experience mix is unknown. Locking the initial position before revealing 16 novices/eight experienced visitors, supported turns and a club-requested competitive finish creates an observable reason to revisit a decision.

## What makes the final decision defensible

- Cup can protect its competitive finish while replacing 10 minutes with orientation and/or adding structured rotations. An extra host would cost $355 and is infeasible
- Open has budget capacity for an extra host ($255), while orientation or rotations may be better justified. Its answer must explain how a credible competitive finish survives the more accessible format
- Showcase can use orientation/rotations to support novices but must acknowledge the access risk of eight stations for 24 visitors. An extra host would cost $315 and is infeasible

The adjustment list should not imply every choice is equally effective. A student needs to connect their chosen adjustment to the revealed audience, retain the 90-minute constraint, and describe what they give up. Rotations have no added financial cost but reduce uninterrupted play; orientation replaces, rather than adds, 10 minutes.

## Pacing and response burden

Three short textareas can support a genuine 10–15 minute individual activity if the visible directions set a short paragraph or two/three sentences per response. Suggested allocation: two minutes to read and choose, three minutes to justify and lock, two minutes to interpret new information, four/five minutes to decide and explain the tradeoff, one minute to review and submit. This is a design estimate, not an observed student timing result.

## Manual rubric

- Initial rationale: 0–2, evidence about the original constraints and uncertainty
- Revised fit: 0–3, concrete use of the audience update and coherent feasible adjustment
- Risk and runner-up tradeoff: 0–3, a named affected stakeholder, plausible risk, mitigation and a real reason not to choose the runner-up
- Adaptation: 0–2, explanation of what changed or why retaining the initial position remains justified

No fixed winning format and no automated correctness score should be implied. A recorded zero is a grade; pending manual review is not zero. Strong retention of the initial format can earn full credit when the reasoning addresses the new information.

## Release review checks

The executed browser suite checks visible sequencing, response count, budget arithmetic, private/manual grade states and all three formats. A reviewer must still read the final wording and conduct a first-time-student pilot to verify that the prompt elicits argument rather than box checking. This lab must be self-contained: no Andrew or guest-speaker contribution is needed to finish or receive a grade.
