SPM 343 Events & VenuesTeaching guide

Ungraded class activity · 30 minutes · pods of 2–3 or solo

Design an esports event.

Give students a reason to choose your event over another night playing at home.

In this fictional brief, La Salle’s student activities team wants a gaming event that strengthens student engagement. Your pod will design the experience, choose a venue, and explain how the pieces fit.

  • 30student attendees
  • 120 minpublic event
  • $1,000confirmed funding
  • 3student organizers
Your task: complete the five sections, download one shared proposal, and prepare a 60-second pitch. Use one device per pod. Everyone helps make and explain the choices.

Your classroom pod may have 1–3 people. The fictional event has three organizers whose jobs you assign.

Use your chapters, slides, and notes. No outside research is needed. Your plan stays on this device; there is no sign-in, online submission, or course grade.

1

Purpose & audience

What are you creating, and for whom?

Choose a specific audience within the student population. For example, newcomers, club competitors, or students looking to meet people.

In one or two sentences, explain the result you want for that audience.

2

Format & experience

What will people actually do?

Explain the main activity and what people do when they are not at a PC. You may name a game or describe the type of play. A detailed tournament bracket or rotation timetable is not needed.

3

Venue selection

Which space fits your event?

Compare the two offers. Either can support a strong plan; explain how your choice serves the experience you designed.

Choose a venue
What both offers include

Each offer includes the room, ready-to-use PCs, a shared viewing screen, setup and teardown time, and one venue technician for equipment problems. The technician does not run your event. Both spaces have 30 attendee places across PC seats and social seating, with a 40-person total occupancy limit that includes staff.

For this case, game permissions, network readiness, accessible routes, and travel arrangements are already confirmed. No extra venue or transport costs are required. Your three student organizers are unpaid. Attendees may play, watch, and socialize; not everyone must use a PC at the same time.

These are fictional classroom offers. They do not describe TAP’s actual prices, equipment, capacity, or services, and do not imply TAP endorsement.

Use two features of your chosen offer to support your event. Name one disadvantage you accept.

5

Evaluation

How will you know it worked?

Connect the target to your purpose. Give a number, the evidence you would collect, and when you would check it. “Everyone has fun” needs a way to be measured.
