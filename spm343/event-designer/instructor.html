SPM 343 · Esports Event DesignerInstructor guide

Run the event planning activity

Ungraded · 30 minutes · Self-selected pods of 2–3 · Solo participation welcome

Students design one event and select a venue that supports it. Each pod uses one device and produces one shared proposal. There are no accounts, required student names, grades, or server submissions. Classroom pods may have 1–3 people; the fictional event has three organizers whose duties students assign.

Open activity

Read to the class

“Design an esports event for 30 student attendees. You have $1,000 and a two-hour public event window. Choose the experience first, then decide which venue makes it work. Explain your staffing, one risk, and how you will judge success. This is ungraded practice. Work in a pod of two or three, or independently, and prepare one proposal.”

Keep these case facts consistent

All offers are fictional classroom assumptions. The $1,000 is confirmed funding; no ticket-sales forecast is needed. Three unpaid student organizers run the event. The 120 minutes cover arrival, activity, and closing; the starting allocation is 15/90/15. Setup and teardown are arranged separately.

Both venues seat all 30 attendees and allow 40 people total, including staff. Each includes one venue technician and a shared screen. Both are accessible; equipment, network access, permissions, and travel are arranged. The technician supports the venue and is not a fourth event organizer.

Optional purchases: coach $150, prizes $200, refreshments $100. Any, all, or none may be selected. The lounge with all three costs $1,100; any two fit. The campus venue with all three costs $800. Students need not spend the full budget.

One proposal with five connected decisions

  1. Purpose and audience: Event name, intended participants, and what the event should accomplish.
  2. Experience and timing: Format, what attendees do, and arrival/activity/closing blocks totaling 120 minutes.
  3. Venue: A choice supported by two case facts and one accepted tradeoff.
  4. Resources and operations: Purchases within $1,000, responsibilities for the three organizers, and one operational risk with a response.
  5. Success: One measurable target, a method for collecting evidence, and a time to check it.

About 150–250 words overall is enough; the app does not enforce a word count. Students can edit in any order. The experience and venue should explain each other.

Give a short pitch

Ask each presenting pod to explain: Who is it for? What happens? Why this venue? What is the key tradeoff? How will you know it worked? Select three different approaches; six minutes is not enough for every pod to present.

Five checks for the discussion

  • Purpose: Is the event designed for a recognizable audience and outcome?
  • Experience: What do attendees do while others use the PCs?
  • Venue fit: Does the equipment and space support the format, and what does the alternative offer?
  • Feasibility: Do time, spending, organizer duties, and the risk response fit together?
  • Evaluation: Would the proposed evidence show whether the stated purpose was achieved?

Use these as discussion prompts, not a rubric. The activity connects the Events chapter’s purpose, format, stakeholders, planning, and evaluation with the Venues chapter’s capacity, equipment, staffing, accessibility, and participant experience. It supports course outcomes 3 and 4.

Optional extension only if time remains

Open the staffing update: one student organizer becomes unavailable, leaving two. The venue technician and any purchased coach remain unchanged. Ask for a two-sentence adjustment identifying a reassigned task and a resulting compromise. Skip this extension during the standard 30-minute session.

Save the work and describe it accurately

The draft saves locally in the same browser. It is not sent to the instructor. Before leaving or clearing a shared device, download the text proposal or use Print and choose Save as PDF. Collect the file only if you announce a collection method.

Suggested syllabus or Canvas note: “Esports Event Designer is an ungraded practice activity completed in self-selected pods or individually. It does not count as one of the four graded Decision Labs. Any later graded assignment will have separate instructions.”
