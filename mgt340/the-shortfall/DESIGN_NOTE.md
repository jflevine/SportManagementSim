# Design note — The Shortfall (MGT 340, college athletics week)

Written for Jeff before building, per `simkit/AUTHORING_GUIDE.md` §1. Jeff was not available in
this session, so the build went ahead on the assumptions listed at the end. **Each one needs
his answer before class use.**

Jeff's assignment: *"MGT 340, college athletics week: students are the athletic director who has
to take $200,000 out of a provisional athletics budget after a sponsor falls through, while a
student-athlete who has heard a rumor about the cut needs an answer before practice."*

## 1. The professional and the moment
Director of Athletics, Wissahickon University (fictional). Tuesday, 1:30 PM. Finance (Ellen
Marsh, AVP Finance) has just emailed: Keystone Mutual has withdrawn a $200,000 sponsorship, so the
$600,000 provisional athlete-experience budget must be resubmitted at exactly $400,000 by
5:00 PM. In the same minute Maya Okonkwo, women's soccer captain and SAAC vice-president, texts:
she heard at lunch the fueling station is being cut entirely; practice is 3:30; SAAC meets at
8:00 and people want to post something. Immovable: practice at 3:30 PM; Finance at 5:00 PM.

## 2. The skill
- Allocate a cut across lines with unequal claims, finding out what cannot move *before* cutting it.
- Decide what to tell a stakeholder before the decision exists — and live with having said it.

## 3. Who holds what
| Person | Wants | Fears | Knows only they know | Has only heard | On their mind today |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Maya Okonkwo (captain, SAAC VP, 21) | Something true to tell 26 teammates at 3:30 | Repeating something false; being "handled" | The fueling station *is breakfast* for early-lift teams (lifts 6 AM, dining 7:30) | That the fueling station is being cut entirely; that Coach Ruiz said so | Practice at 3:30, SAAC at 8 |
| Ellen Marsh (AVP Finance) | Exactly $400,000, savings that are real | A budget that balances on paper only | Cancelling the $40,000 fall charter forfeits all of it | — | The 5 PM deadline |
| Renee Coles (Assoc. AD, Student-Athlete Services) | Every academic progress plan funded | An athlete losing eligibility over unfunded hours | 38 athletes on plans; their hours cost ≥ $66,000. Psychologist can drop to 0.25 FTE ($35k) | — | Nobody has asked what her lines can bear |
| Tony Ruiz (men's basketball coach) | The charter travel upgrade | Long buses; looking weak to recruits | He started the rumour; he has pitched the charter to two recruits (both guarded) | — | Protecting travel |

## 4. What can be verified, and where
- **Line-item worksheet**: amounts; that $40,000 of travel is a *signed* charter. Does not say cancelling forfeits it (Ellen).
- **Department policy 4.2** (fictional): plans require six funded tutor hours a week. Does not say how many athletes or what it costs (Renee).
- **Fueling station usage record**: 410 visits/day, 62% before 9 AM. Does not say *why* (Maya).

## 5. What a reasonable person might get wrong
- Reassure Maya ("the fueling station is not being cut, I promise") and keep the promise by cutting a line whose claimants were never asked (tutoring below the plan floor).
- Tell her it's "just a rumour, nobody is cutting anything" to calm things before practice; she repeats it to the team and SAAC, and the 5 PM budget contradicts it.
- Work the numbers alone and say nothing; the rumour stands at practice, SAAC posts it; or cut travel below the signed charter and have Finance return it.

## 6. Who relies on the decision
Maya repeats what she was told at 3:30 (team, then SAAC chat). SAAC decides at 8:00 whether to
post, and what. Finance accepts or returns the plan at 5:00. Renee acts on the tutoring number
Wednesday morning. The fueling station opens Wednesday at 6:00 AM on whatever is left.
Students find out in the debrief after submitting.

## 7. The debrief
Side by side: **Team Alder** (promised the fueling station was safe, kept the promise, unfunded
the academic progress plans without knowing) and **Team Birch** (told Maya a cut was coming and
nothing was final, asked what mattered, asked Renee and Ellen what could not move, showed Maya the
final draft before Finance). Question: *"One AD gave Maya certainty at 2 PM; the other told her a
cut was coming and nothing was final. Which answer could she repeat at practice, and which could
the AD still stand behind at 8 PM?"* Follow-up on the tutoring line: *"Before you cut a line, whose
job is it to tell you what the line cannot lose — theirs, or yours to ask?"*

## Interaction format, and why
A **budget board** (move amounts, see the running total against $400,000, save dated drafts,
choose whom to show a draft to) because the professional act is allocation; claimants react only
to what they have been shown. A **text/email column** beside it, because the captain's message is
the interruption that competes for the same afternoon, and what an AD says to her is the second
skill. Not The Report's desk: there is no inbox to triage, and the board is the work.

## Assumptions made without Jeff (please confirm or correct)
1. **Numbers and lines.** I kept the $600,000 → $400,000 envelope from the existing
   `mgt340/chavez-decision` exercise (Chapter 8 Chavez adaptation) but replaced its five
   categories (institutional payments, aid, support, facilities, reserve) with seven concrete
   lines. If the chapter's categories are course vocabulary you want students to use, the
   lines should change back. All dollar figures, the 38 athletes, the $66,000 floor and the
   usage record are invented for the fiction.
2. **No real law or NCAA/conference rule.** "Academic progress plans" and their eligibility
   consequence are a fictional *department policy* (4.2), not a real academic-progress rule. I
   avoided institutional athlete payments, revenue sharing and Title IX entirely so nothing
   needs legal verification; if the week's outcomes include gender-equity reasoning, that is a
   deliberate gap to discuss.
3. **Time in class.** Built for roughly 25–35 minutes of play in pods sharing one seat (the
   clock runs 1:30–5:00 PM of simulated time; each action costs 5–10 minutes), plus a debrief.
4. **Pods.** One device per pod with teammates joining by team code, as in The Report.
5. **What students struggled with last time.** Unknown. I designed against the audit's
   description of the old AD exercise (promises to the athlete were not recorded and the shock
   hit every team alike). If students actually struggled with something else, the lenses should
   change.
6. **Lines can only go down.** A line cannot be raised above its provisional amount (simpler
   board, keeps it a cut). Easy to relax if Jeff wants reallocation.
7. **Names.** Wissahickon University, Keystone Mutual, and all four people are invented; the
   Philadelphia flavour is deliberate and harmless, but any resemblance to a real local
   institution should be checked.
