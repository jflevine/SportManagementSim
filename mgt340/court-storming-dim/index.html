FACILITATION

A 15-minute D.I.M. exercise

Students act as the host university’s event manager. The 2024 Clark collision launches a fictional next-game simulation. Students develop a plan, assign functional teams, make three decisions, and revise their plan.

  1. 2 minutes: Read the case and choose solo or pair mode.
  2. 4 minutes: Develop: choose a priority risk and two measures; explain the fit in one or two sentences. Implement: assign all three units, an activation signal, and communication.
  3. 5 minutes: Manage: advance through 30 seconds remaining, the final buzzer, and departure. Read the consequence after each decision.
  4. 4 minutes: Review process evidence, write a revision, export for Canvas, and debrief.

What changes in the simulation?

The exit-route measure helps establish an available lane. A rehearsal plus named radio confirmations establishes readiness. Spectator guidance slows the fictional crowd’s approach but does not cover an empty post. A verified backup enables the alternate-route choice. Staff placement, activation, and in-game decisions also change the displayed operation.

What to listen for

Specific roles, coverage tradeoffs, observable triggers, confirmation of instructions, and revision based on evidence. The final result is not a numeric safety score or an automatic grade of written reasoning. Choosing a priority group frames reflection; it does not change the duty to consider other people.

Debrief question

If nobody is injured, is that enough evidence that your plan worked?

Ask a pair that concentrated staff at the exit to compare its experience with a pair that kept posts covered. Then ask: “What did you have to fix during the event that you could have prepared beforehand?” Use the recorded warnings even if a team eventually completed departure.

Collecting work

Students enter names, complete a short revision, then download or copy the full report for Canvas. Print / Save PDF is also available. The report includes setup, all three decisions, consequences, process evidence, and reflection. Work stays on the device; there is no central dashboard or automatic submission. No opening or unlocking is required.

Trying another approach

“Restart simulation” retains the setup and names, then unlocks edits. It clears the current decisions and reflection after confirmation. Export a completed run first if it should be retained. “Start over” clears the new activity. Earlier writing-version plans remain separately available from the case screen.

The diagram, three functional units, score, crowd movement, and outcomes are teaching simplifications. They are not Ohio State’s venue, staffing recommendations, injury predictions, or findings of legal fault. The AP source describes a collision without a reported injury.
