The Saturday Decision Desk

MGT 340 · 10-minute pod lab · Facilitator / no-internet fallback

Open the interactive activity · Print this page for a paper fallback.

The brief

Your pod leads fictional Fairmount Community Soccer: 180 players ages 9–12, a $450 season fee before optional costs, and a one-time $12,000 improvement budget. Regular operations and required safety procedures are already funded. Mission: safe, enjoyable, accessible development.

Roles: director, family/player advocate, finance/operations lead. A fourth participant challenges assumptions. Use one device per pod. No personal information or firsthand disclosure is needed.

Pacing: 1 minute brief, 6 minutes for three decisions, 1 minute board handoff, 2 minutes debrief. No countdown, scores or ranked winner.

Paper fallback: circle one option in each round, add costs, stay at or below $12,000, and write the three handoff responses below. The options and numbers match the website.

All league details, prices, tradeoffs and situations are invented teaching assumptions, not research estimates or predicted outcomes.

1. Who gets to stay in the game?

Registration opens next week. Families report three barriers: the $450 season fee, transport to practice, and paying the whole fee at once. You can fund one access priority this season.

A. Reduce the fee barrier

$6,000

Offer 20 confidential, need-based $300 fee credits. Publish eligibility and a simple request process.

Tradeoff: Deep support for 20 players; transport and families just above the threshold still need a plan.

B. Make attendance possible

$3,000

Fund an approved transport partner for a limited route serving practice and local games.

Tradeoff: Helps families on that route, but does not lower registration fees or reach every neighborhood.

C. Spread the payment

$1,000

Set up no-interest installment plans and clear all-in price information before registration.

Tradeoff: Eases timing and surprise costs. It does not reduce the total fee for families who cannot afford it.

2. What counts as development?

Some parents want early “elite” labels and more travel. Coaches see late developers and children who want to play other sports. Choose one development investment; the regular local season remains available to everyone.

A. Reopen the pathway

$2,000

Add two open reassessment dates with common criteria and a second trained evaluator.

Tradeoff: More chances and better process, but extra assessment can still create pressure and labeling.

B. Build skills without a label

$4,000

Offer eight optional, no-fee mixed-ability skill sessions with flexible attendance for multi-sport players.

Tradeoff: Broad skill access; paid coaching time is expensive and some families want a selective pathway.

C. Improve everyday coaching

$1,000

Train volunteer coaches on age-appropriate goals, varied practice and clear parent communication.

Tradeoff: Reaches regular sessions cheaply, but training alone does not ensure behavior changes.

3. The sideline crosses a line

At Saturday’s game, an adult repeatedly shouts at a youth official and moves toward the field after being told to stop. A child says they no longer want to play. Apply the safety baseline, then choose one prevention investment.

Mandatory safety baseline: pause play when safety is in doubt; move children and the official away from the confrontation; use the designated trained adult and league protocol. Children do not intervene. Use the emergency plan and emergency services for immediate danger. Document facts, support those affected, follow safeguarding/reporting duties, and use published conduct review, fair notice and appeal procedures. Safety is never a budget option.

A. Staff the busiest games

$4,000

Add trained, paid game-day stewards at the highest-attendance fixtures, separate from coaches.

Tradeoff: Clear authority and reliable cover at selected games; other fixtures still rely on existing staff.

B. Build a trained rotation

$2,000

Train and schedule adult volunteer sideline hosts, with a designated supervisor and backup each day.

Tradeoff: Wider coverage at lower cost, but availability and consistent enforcement need active management.

C. Support officials & reporting

$3,000

Add an adult official mentor, a clear reporting route and post-game follow-up for affected participants.

Tradeoff: Stronger support and visibility; no extra steward at every game. Reports may rise as trust improves.

Board handoff

Choices: _____ / _____ / _____    Total: $________ of $12,000    Reserve: $________

1. Who is still underserved, and what will you do about it?

2. Who owns your first implementation step? Use a league role and concrete action before next Saturday.

3. What will you check after 30 days, and what result would trigger a change?

Facilitate the debrief

Ask two pods with different bundles for 30-second pitches. Compare whose needs are met, what each gives up, and what evidence could change the decision. Do not ask students to disclose family financial or traumatic experiences.

  • Fee plans change timing, not total affordability. Support should reach families who do not apply or cannot use the transport route.
  • Multiple assessment points can widen access, but performance labels can still create pressure. Coaching training needs observation, not merely completion.
  • Safety is non-negotiable. Prevention choices need clear responsibility, coverage and fair follow-up. Higher reporting may mean more trust rather than more harm.
  • Link choices to mission and to planning, organizing, leading and evaluating. An input (dollars or training hours) is not an outcome (enjoyment, inclusion or retention).

Example feasible bundles: fee credits + reassessment + paid stewards = $12,000; transport + open clinics + reporting support = $10,000; payment plans + coach training + volunteer rotation = $4,000, but much more unmet need may remain. These are discussion examples, not answer keys. Fee credits + open clinics + paid stewards costs $14,000 and must be revised.
